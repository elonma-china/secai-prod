In the identification phase, administrators collect logs from firewalls, web servers, and intrusion detection systems to determine whether it is a real incident or just a false alarm. The operations team should closely monitor the system for at least seventy-two hours after recovery to detect any signs of re-infection early.\n\nFinally, the lessons learned step is where the entire incident is documented in a detailed report: the time it was discovered, the time it was resolved, the root causes, the estimated damage, and the preventive measures to be implemented in the future. Companies that maintain the habit of conducting regular audits, fixing vulnerabilities in a timely manner, and providing security awareness training to their employees will generally see a significant reduction in serious incidents over the long term. No system is absolutely secure, but a clear response procedure that is practiced regularly can help minimize downtime and damage when an incident does occur. ra.\n\nAlongside the incident response process, patch management is another key pillar of an enterprise’s information security program. Whenever a software vendor releases a new patch, the operations team needs to assess the severity of the vulnerability based on the CVSS score, the scope of impact, and the likelihood of it being exploited in practice before scheduling its deployment. Vulnerabilities with a score of Those rated nine and above with publicly available exploit codes need to be fixed within seventy-two hours, while medium-severity vulnerabilities can be scheduled for the regular monthly maintenance cycle.\n\nBefore deploying the patch to the production environment, the technical team should test it in a staging environment with a similar configuration to identify compatibility issues early. A patch applied hastily without testing can cause more severe service disruptions than the vulnerability it aims to fix. All changes need to be recorded in the change management system, including the version. before and after the patch, the person performing it, the deployment time, and the recovery plan if the patch causes errors.\n\nFor systems that cannot be patched immediately for operational reasons, the security team needs to implement temporary mitigation measures such as network isolation, access restrictions, or enabling specialized intrusion detection rules for that vulnerability, while closely monitoring them until a formal patch is applied. The list of IT assets needs to be updated regularly for the operations team. Always know exactly which software version is running where, to avoid missing servers when scanning for vulnerabilities.\n\nA regular report on the status of patching should be presented to management on a quarterly basis, including the number of critical vulnerabilities remaining, the average time taken to patch them, and trends compared to the previous period. An effective patch management program not only helps reduce the attack surface but also serves as important evidence when the company needs to demonstrate compliance with information security standards to partners. or the management team. Investing appropriately in automated vulnerability scanning and patch deployment will help the technical team save a significant amount of time compared to handling each server manually.\n "
